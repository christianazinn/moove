## Inspiration
*(TODO humans: replace this paragraph's first lines with our own moving story, ideally tied to the Anthropic community below.)*

Moving is mostly admin. You decide what to keep, look up what everything is worth, list things one at a time, haggle, donate the rest, then buy whatever the new place is missing. It takes days, and the easy way out is a dumpster. Agents should be good at this kind of work, but nobody wants an agent holding their card with no supervision. So we built a move where agents do the tedious parts, and you keep the final say on money and on anything that matters to you.

## What it does
Moove is an iPhone app with five tabs that follow the move: **Scan, Items, Sell & Buy, Move, Summary**.

1. **Scan.** Walk the room with the phone's LiDAR (Apple RoomPlan). An on-device detector catches what RoomPlan doesn't know (bikes, plants, guitars, suitcases), LiDAR measures each one, and each object gets a photo cut from the scan's own frames. The room comes back as a **voxel dollhouse**: real walls, doors and windows, each piece where it stood, colored from the camera frames. The back walls stand full height and the near walls are cut low, and they swap as you turn the room. The Scan home shows your latest room slowly turning. While you scan, what the detector finds shows as "Also found" chips (newest first), and before import you can name any piece it couldn't (**Name it**).
2. **Identify.** Claude vision reads the photos plus the LiDAR size and fills in name, brand, material and condition, and flags anything fragile or possibly sentimental. A room-photo sweep names the boxes the scanner couldn't label and adds things it missed. When Claude can't tell which model a valuable item is, the app asks for the one close-up that would settle it, like the sticker on the back. **Items > Inventory** is a little museum: items grouped into halls by what they're for (Sleep, Sit & lounge, Work & study, Kitchen & dining…), each a voxel exhibit on a plinth with a short placard (name, brand and size, a one-line story, price). Tap room cards to focus on one or two scans. A misread piece is fixed in seconds (Rename, What is it?, Turn around, Not a real item), and you can add an item by hand. Parts of the building (toilets, sinks, tubs, radiators) stay out of the item lists and are never priced or listed for sale; the 3D room still draws them.
3. **Price.** A pricing agent pulls comparable sold and local listings through **Apify** (eBay sold, Facebook Marketplace) and sets an estimate with a range. It pays for that data itself, $0.01 per lookup over **x402** in USDC on Base Sepolia, from a CDP wallet with its own budget. *(Our free Apify credit is used up until Nov 23, so today the comps are labeled replays of earlier real scrapes.)*
4. **Sort.** Moove suggests keep, sell, donate or trash, with a reason for each. It never overrides you, and possibly sentimental items wait for you to decide. **Items > Review** puts those in a **Needs you** card deck (swipe right to keep, left to sell, up to donate, down to trash, with Undo), over a **Where it goes** board: four moving-box labels (Keep, Sell, Donate, Trash) with a count, a total and the items' voxel models; tap one for its list and move anything to another pile.
5. **Sell.** A listing agent drafts the eBay listing with Claude. **Sell & Buy > Listings** is a wall of posters, one per listing, with the item's voxel model, the price, a status stamp (NEEDS YOUR OK, POSTED, SOLD…) and tear-off tabs that read "eBay · simulated" until a post is real. Anything over your approval limit waits for you, either as an Auth0 push to your phone (CIBA) or under **Needs your OK** on **Sell & Buy > Agents**, the one screen for what your agents want and what they spent. A safety agent can also pay a third-party x402 service for a CPSC recall check on the item's brand first, and a probable recall makes the listing ask you. *(The eBay post is simulated and labeled. The recall check is opt-in and not on the hosted worker yet.)*
6. **Buy.** Claude drafts what the new home still needs. A buy agent finds deals, screens each one for fraud, and checks out through a Visa Acceptance flow, with your approval above the limit. **Sell & Buy > Shopping** is a storefront sorted by category (the same halls as the Inventory museum): a hero with the best saving, then a section per category with big item cards and its own row of deals, and a **Blocked for your safety** section at the end. Under the limit, **Choose** asks you to confirm before it pays. It refuses any deal scoring 0.7 or higher. In the demo, a planted scam (a "brand new" $95 Aeron, Zelle only, labeled as not a real listing) scores 0.85 and is blocked. *(Checkout is a real Authorize.net **sandbox** charge on the public test card, no real money, labeled VISA SANDBOX.)*
7. **Move.** Paste a Zillow, Redfin or Realtor.com link, or use the demo house. Moove reads the address from the link, and Claude reads room sizes off any floor-plan screenshots you add. Without screenshots, it estimates a plan from beds, baths and square feet. You pick which scanned rooms are moving (the newest scan of each by default). Everything you kept from them flies from its spot in the old room into the new home, along with what the buy agent bought, in one of three layouts placed by interior-design rules: one bed per bedroom with nightstands either side, the sofa facing the TV, chairs around the table, walkways kept clear, and a bought mattress on the kept frame. **Room shots** are photos of each furnished room on its own, to share. **Pack** puts what's moving into fewer, fuller numbered boxes (9 on the demo), shown as a grid of big box cards colored by destination room, with a reason for each box, each box's contents in 3D, and printable labels with QR codes. The Truck and crew card shows the loaded voxel truck right there (**Load the truck** replays the load order). Things you bought are listed as delivered to the new home, not packed.
8. **Summary.** What went where, what you earned and spent (real and simulated kept apart), roughly how many kg stayed out of landfill, and an **"Agents kept you safe"** card: scams blocked, approvals asked, sentimental items left to you, and spend against each budget.

Every agent step and every payment is a row in SpacetimeDB, so **Sell & Buy > Agents** shows a live spending log as paper receipts you swipe through sideways, one per agent run: the amount, the agent, the purpose, the budget left and a Basescan link. Anything that isn't real gets a SIMULATED badge. Each tab has its own color (sage Scan, ochre Items, terracotta Sell & Buy, teal Move, plum Summary), and Settings > Appearance switches between System, Light and Dark.

**What's real and what's simulated:** real today are the scan, Claude vision and listing copy, the Apify comps (labeled replays of real scrapes right now, because our free Apify credit is used up until Nov 23; the code searches live when there's credit), SpacetimeDB sync, the fraud rules, and x402 settlement on Base Sepolia ([tx](https://sepolia.basescan.org/tx/0xc7942646380456c56dde204434aeb6115338b521b6e91a0901ec05dde1fe136c)): since the hosted worker got CDP keys on Saturday, its new pricing lookups settle for real ([tx](https://sepolia.basescan.org/tx/0x698e3721c1df84e3c183ec2bfebca3037c26fc511b9c513c7f94064be2f03031)). These are simulated and labeled in the data and the app: the eBay posts, Visa purchases from before Sat ~1:50 PM, and x402 rows written before the hosted worker got CDP keys. The Auth0 CIBA push is live on the hosted worker (tested against our real tenant). The AI-written-text fraud signal is off. ## How we built it
- **iOS app** (SwiftUI, Swift 6.2): RoomPlan for the scan, an on-device YOLOE detector (127 household classes) with LiDAR depth for objects RoomPlan misses, and RealityKit for the dollhouse and the fly-in. A pure-Swift `MooveKit` package holds the models, the layout solver (interior-design rules for groups, facing and clearances, with a walkway check from the doors), voxel builder, scan color sampling, packing planner and summary math, all unit tested (they also run on Linux).
- **Voxel models, no downloaded assets.** Every item is a 5 cm voxel model built on the phone from its own data. RoomPlan's attributes pick the shape first, then the words from Claude vision, then the scanned size, with builders for nearly every detector category (bikes, guitars, strollers, cribs, bins and more). A shape check redraws a mislabeled piece by its measured shape (a "pillow" that's a tall thin pole is drawn as a lamp). Condition adds wear, and the colors come from the scan.
- **SpacetimeDB is the whole backend** (a TypeScript module). Tables are the shared state, and reducers are the only way to write. The app and the agent worker never talk to each other directly. Both subscribe. Room and user rows are private tables, read through per-user views. The iOS client uses the community Swift SDK, which we vetted in an hour-zero spike (~1.5 ms live update between two clients).
- **Agent worker** (Node/TS) holds all outbound I/O. Each agent watches for rows that need work and writes back through reducers: vision, room-photo sweep and listing copy (Claude), pricing (Apify plus x402), approvals (Auth0 CIBA), shopping list (Claude), buy (Apify deal search plus Visa) and fraud screening (rules). Each integration sits behind an interface with a labeled simulated fallback, so the demo never depends on one flaky API.
- **x402 rail:** `@coinbase/cdp-sdk` (`CdpX402Client`, `createX402Server`), `@x402/*` v2 and the CDP facilitator. We drive the 402 flow by hand, so every call carries its agent and purpose and the budget is checked before anything is signed.
- **Guardrails in code:** per-agent x402 budgets and a per-call max, a card budget and a per-purchase max, the fraud block, approval thresholds, approvals that expire and ask again, and daily caps on our own Claude and Apify spend.
- **Proof on every PR:** `npm run e2e -- --replay` runs 22 stages on a local SpacetimeDB (scan, pricing, sort, sweep, listing, approval, shopping, deals, fraud, purchase, sharing, privacy) with replayed answers and no paid calls. XCUITests tap through the app in an iOS Simulator.
- **Team:** four people and their Claude Code agents, coordinating through GitHub issues and PRs. The data-model doc is the contract between lanes, and decisions are recorded in `docs/decisions/`.

## Challenges we ran into
- **No official Swift SDK for SpacetimeDB.** We spiked the community SDK in the first hour instead of building a gateway. It works, but it has quirks: it needs Swift 6.2, initial rows arrive as inserts, and it doesn't replay subscriptions after a reconnect.
- **x402 packaging.** `@coinbase/cdp-sdk/x402` crashes on import unless you install its optional peer dependencies by hand, and the buyer-side docs don't say how to get the settlement tx hash. We found it by reading the type definitions.
- **Per-agent budgets.** The SDK's hooks apply to the whole client, so we wrote a reserve/release ledger that stops one agent's concurrent calls from overspending.
- **Restarts in the middle of a payment.** A restart between a card hold and its capture left the hold open, and a restart after an approval request asked twice. Now holds left by a crashed run are reversed, approval requests are reused, and a listing is marked "posting" before it's published, so it's never posted twice.
- **Real rooms are messy.** On a real iPad scan, detector boxes sat too close to the camera, faced backwards and duplicated RoomPlan's own boxes, and a lamp came back as a "pillow". We re-centered and turned the boxes using the camera position and the walls, merged sightings across views, and let the measured shape overrule a wrong name.
- **Sandboxes that need onboarding.** eBay, Auth0 CIBA and Visa Acceptance all need accounts, keys or plan features before a single real call. CyberSource's sandbox accepted our signed calls but failed with HTTP 502 on every merchant, so we added an Authorize.net sandbox backend (also Visa Acceptance) that charges for real in sandbox. We built each integration against stubbed HTTP and kept a labeled simulation.
- **LiDAR on stage.** RoomPlan needs a physical LiDAR device, so we keep a recorded demo scan, a seeded demo room and a demo house as fallbacks.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of
- A real x402 payment from an agent's CDP wallet, settled on Base Sepolia, with the budget checked before signing. The pricing agent buys its comps through the same rail.
- A paid call to an x402 service we don't run: a $0.002 CPSC recall check from the CDP x402 Bazaar, through the same budgets and payee allowlist, settled three times on Base Sepolia.
- Agents that coordinate only through database tables, with every step visible to the user as it happens.
- Guardrails that say no, proved on every PR: x402 calls past the budget are refused before signing, a $429.99 purchase over the card budget is refused, and a scam deal scored 0.85 is refused with no approval and no charge.
- Honesty built into the data: `simulated: true` on every fake payment or listing, `[SIMULATED]` on every simulated money step in the log, and badges the UI can't hide.
- Your scanned room rebuilt as a voxel dollhouse, each piece at its scanned spot and in its own color, then flown into a new home whose floor plan came from a listing link and furnished by interior-design rules, with a photo of each furnished room.
- One place to supervise the agents: **Sell & Buy > Agents** stacks what's waiting for your OK, what you decided, and every payment as a receipt, real and simulated kept apart.
- Privacy in the database itself: a room can only be joined with a code its owner shared, and join codes and emails aren't world-readable.

## What we learned
- Agents are much easier to trust when every action is a row you can see, not a log line on a server.
- The useful payment primitive for agents is a per-agent budget with reserve and release, plus a fetch that returns the receipt. Signing is the easy part.
- Contract-first planning (one data-model doc, one schema file) let four lanes and their agents build in parallel against fixtures.
- Label simulated actions from day one. Retrofitting honesty into a demo is much harder.
- Trust the measurement over the label. A LiDAR box size is right far more often than any model's name for it.

### Spacetime
SpacetimeDB isn't an add-on in Moove. It's the only backend. The iOS app and the agent worker never talk to each other: both subscribe to the same tables and write only through reducers. The agents coordinate through rows. Vision fills in an item, pricing picks it up, then sorting, listing and approval, and each step writes an `agent_event` that the app shows live. Payments and refusals are rows too, which is what makes the live spending log work. Shared rooms (`join_room` with a 6-character code) let two phones review one inventory. Authorization uses `ctx.sender`, and `room`/`user` are private tables read through per-user views. Code: `spacetime/src/index.ts`, `docs/data-model.md`. **Honest status:** the iOS mapping of every table was checked against a local server, not yet on a device, and share/join hasn't been tested on two real phones. **Show judges:** a scan's items appearing in Items > Inventory (the museum halls), then names and prices filling in as the agents finish; Share room / Join a room (`4RZ9G8` on the Demo backend) if two phones are on hand; `npm run e2e -- --replay` (22 stages, ~15 s).

### Visa: Reimagining Shopping
Moving is a shopping problem: the new place needs a bed frame, a desk and a shower curtain, and nobody wants to spend a weekend comparing listings. Moove's buy agent drafts what's missing from the new home's floor plan, minus what you're keeping. It ranks deals and screens each one for fraud (price outliers, off-platform payment, new sellers, duplicates), and shows the reasons. It never buys a deal scoring 0.7 or higher, and it waits on any deal that isn't scored yet. What you buy lands in the new home's 3D layout and on Pack's "Delivered to your new home" list. **Honest status:** checkout is a real **sandbox** charge (Authorize.net sandbox, test card, no real money; rows `sandbox: true`, approvals say VISA SANDBOX), live since Sat ~1:50 PM; the first one on Live is a $89.99 Target bed frame, sandbox transaction 120092631489. CyberSource's sandbox still returns HTTP 502. VIC is a documented seam only. **Show judges:** Sell & Buy > Shopping (the storefront's **Blocked for your safety** section), then Desk chair (the planted Zelle-only Aeron, "Likely fraud · 0.85", "Blocked: likely fraud"), then **Choose** on the Target chair and the confirm ("Pay $79.99 to target"), then the sofa's approval card, then the bought pieces flying into the new home.

### Auth0 (MLH)
Auth0 for AI Agents is the human in the loop for spending. When an agent wants to list or buy something above your limit, the worker sends an async authorization (CIBA) request: a Guardian push to your phone with a binding message like `Moove: sell 420.00 USD: Herman Miller chair`. The amount comes first, so a scraped title can't pose as the price. The worker checks that the token's subject is you before it proceeds. It never asks twice after a restart, and a request nobody answers expires after 12 hours and is asked again at most twice. Without push set up, the request waits under **Needs your OK** on the app's Sell & Buy > Agents screen. The app signs in with Auth0 Universal Login, renews an expired ID token, and uses the ID token as your SpacetimeDB identity. Code: `agents/src/integrations/auth0.ts`, `agents/src/agents/approval.ts`, `ios/Moove/Features/Auth/`. **Honest status:** CIBA is live on the hosted worker and was tested against our real tenant on Saturday. The push goes to the approval's owner when they're signed in with Auth0. Guests, and the seeded Demo room's owner, get the in-app inbox instead. **Show judges:** Sell & Buy > **Agents** ("Needs your OK · 2", Approve on the $410 Aeron listing, the EXPIRED request under Decided) and the Auth0 login screen.

### Best Planet-First Hack
Moove's default is resell or donate, not trash. Every keep/sell/donate/trash suggestion comes with a reason, and the Summary shows roughly how many kg stayed out of landfill (labeled as an estimate). **Show judges:** Summary tab ("Kept from landfill", "Where everything went").
