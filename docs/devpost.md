## Inspiration
*(TODO humans: replace with our own moving story, ideally tied to the Anthropic community below.)* Moving is mostly admin. You decide what to keep, look up what everything is worth, list things one by one, haggle, donate the rest, and then buy what the new place is missing. It takes days, and the easy way out is a dumpster. Agents should be good at this kind of work, but nobody wants an agent with their card and no supervision. So we wanted to see what a move looks like when agents do the tedious parts and the user keeps the final say on money and on anything that matters to them.

## What it does
1. **Scan.** You scan a room with the iPhone's LiDAR (RoomPlan). An on-device detector catches what RoomPlan doesn't know (bikes, scooters, plants, suitcases...) and LiDAR measures each one, and every object gets a photo cut from the scan's own frames, so you only retake the bad ones. When you finish, the Scan page shows your room as a voxel dollhouse: the real walls, doors and windows, each piece where it stood, colored from the scan's own camera frames. *(A recorded demo scan, "Load demo scan", goes through the same import path and shows the same dollhouse, labeled as a demo.)* The colors are saved on each item, so the new home uses them too.
2. **Identify.** Claude vision looks at the photos plus the scan's dimensions and fills in the name, brand, material, condition and whether it's fragile. It also flags items that might be sentimental, and those are never decided automatically.
3. **Price.** A pricing agent pulls comparable sold and local listings through Apify (eBay sold listings, Facebook Marketplace) and sets an estimate with a low/high range. It pays for that data itself, $0.01 per lookup over **x402** in USDC on Base Sepolia, from a CDP wallet with a per-agent budget.
4. **Review.** You confirm keep / sell / donate / trash on a review screen. Possibly sentimental items sit in their own section until you decide. Moove pre-sorts with rules (value vs. moving cost, condition, category) and never overrides you.
5. **Sell.** A listing agent writes the eBay listing and posts it. Anything above your approval threshold waits for you: an Auth0 push to your phone (CIBA), or the in-app Approvals inbox.
6. **Buy.** For the new home, Claude drafts what's still missing, a buy agent searches deals, scores them for fraud risk, and checks out through a Visa Acceptance (CyberSource) flow, with approval above the threshold. It won't buy a deal that scores 0.7 or higher: the demo's planted scam deal (a "brand new" $95 Aeron, Zelle only, labeled as not a real listing) scores 0.85 and is refused. Selling and buying live in the app's **Sell & Buy** tab (Listings, Shopping, Approvals, Spending). *(The demo checkout is the labeled simulation.)*
7. **New home.** Paste a Zillow, Redfin or Realtor.com link. Moove reads the address from the link without opening the page. Add floor-plan screenshots and Claude reads the room sizes from them; without them, Moove estimates a plan from beds, baths and square feet. You can also type room sizes or use the demo house. *(An Apify Zillow scrape is an opt-in switch for the demo; it's off by default.)*
8. **Pack.** The Pack tab turns what you're keeping into numbered boxes labeled by the room they're going to, furniture notes, a supplies list, a packing schedule, and a rough truck, crew and cost estimate.
9. **Move in.** Everything you kept, plus what the buy agent bought, flies into the new home as voxel models, in one of three layout styles (by function, compact, open). Save the one you like.
10. **Summary.** What went where, what you earned and spent (real vs. simulated), and roughly how much stayed out of landfill.

Every agent step and every payment is a row in SpacetimeDB, so the app shows a live spending log: amount, agent, purpose, remaining budget, a basescan link, and a SIMULATED badge on anything that isn't real. Every simulated money step in the log starts with `[SIMULATED]`. *(The live backend maps `payment` and `agent_event` since #28; it's pending verification on a device. The Offline demo shows the same screen on fixture data.)*

## How we built it
- **iOS app** (SwiftUI, Swift 6): RoomPlan for the scan, RealityKit for the 3D new home and the fly-in, and a pure-Swift `MooveKit` package for the models, layout solver, voxel models, scan color sampling, packing plan and summary math, so they're unit tested on Linux too.
- **Voxel models**: every item is a 5 cm voxel model generated on the phone from its own data. RoomPlan's attributes (chair, sofa, table, storage type) decide the shape first, then the name, material and brand words from Claude vision, then the scanned size; the condition adds wear, and colors come from the scan's camera frames. No 3D asset is downloaded. The project is generated with XcodeGen.
- **SpacetimeDB** (TypeScript module) is the whole backend. Tables are the shared state and reducers are the only writes. The app and the agent worker are both just subscribers. The iOS client uses the community Swift SDK (`ekscrypto/spacetimedb-swift-sdk`), which we vetted in an hour-0 spike (~1.5 ms live update between two clients).
- **Agent worker** (Node/TS) holds all outbound I/O. Each agent subscribes to rows that need work and writes results back through reducers: vision (Claude), pricing (Apify), listing (Claude + eBay Sell API sandbox), approval (Auth0 CIBA), shopping list (Claude), buy (Apify deal search + Visa checkout) and fraud screening (rules, plus an optional Pangram AI-text hook). Each integration sits behind an interface with a labeled simulated fallback, so the demo never depends on one flaky API.
- **x402 payment rail**: `@coinbase/cdp-sdk` (`CdpX402Client`, `createX402Server`), `@x402/*` v2 and the CDP facilitator. We drive the 402 flow by hand so each call carries its agent and purpose, and so the budget check happens before anything is signed. Details: [`docs/coinbase-x402.md`](coinbase-x402.md).
- **Coordination**: four people plus their Claude Code agents, who coordinated through GitHub issues and PRs. Every task has a brief in `docs/tasks/`, and the data model doc is the contract between lanes.

## Challenges we ran into
- **No official Swift SDK for SpacetimeDB.** We spiked the community SDK in the first hour instead of building a gateway. It worked, with quirks: it needs Swift 6.2, initial rows arrive as inserts, and it doesn't replay subscriptions on reconnect. Every table is now mapped into the app (#28); sharing a room between two phones (#78) is built but not yet tested on two real phones.
- **x402 packaging.** `@coinbase/cdp-sdk/x402` crashes on import unless its optional peer dependencies are installed by hand, and its CJS/ESM type split needed a cast. The buyer-side docs also don't say how to get the settlement tx hash, so we read the type definitions to find it.
- **Per-agent budgets.** The SDK's hooks apply to the whole client, so we built our own reserve/release ledger to keep concurrent calls from one agent from overspending.
- **Sandboxes that need onboarding.** eBay, Auth0 CIBA and Visa Acceptance all need accounts, keys or plan features before a single real call. We built each against stubbed HTTP and kept a labeled simulation, so the demo is honest about what's real.
- **Restarts in the middle of a payment.** A worker restart between a card hold and its capture left the hold open, and a restart after asking for approval asked again. The worker now reverses holds left by an earlier run, reuses the earlier approval request, and never pays a deal the fraud screen hasn't scored yet (#138).
- **Making a generic model look like *your* item.** A box from RoomPlan says "chair, 0.6 × 0.6 × 1.0 m". We combined RoomPlan's attributes, the vision agent's words and colors projected from the scan's camera frames so the voxel piece reads as the actual object, and kept randomness to small details only.
- **Lidar on stage.** RoomPlan needs a physical LiDAR device, so we keep a demo scan and a hardcoded demo house as fallbacks.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of
- A real x402 payment from an agent's CDP wallet, settled on Base Sepolia, with the budget checked before signing ([tx](https://sepolia.basescan.org/tx/0xc7942646380456c56dde204434aeb6115338b521b6e91a0901ec05dde1fe136c)). Pricing now pays for its comps through the same rail.
- Agents that coordinate only through database tables, with every step visible to the user as it happens.
- Guardrails built into the data model: spend caps, an approval threshold, sentimental items left to the user, and `simulated` labels that the UI can't hide.
- Privacy in the database itself: room and user rows are private tables that each phone reads only through its own views (`my_room`, `my_user`), so a room can only be joined with a code its owner shared.
- One command proves the pipeline: `npm run e2e -- --replay` runs scan → pricing → sort → sweep → listing → approval → shopping → deals → fraud → purchase → sharing → privacy on a local SpacetimeDB in about 15 seconds, on every PR.
- An end-to-end test in CI that runs the whole pipeline on replayed data and proves the guardrails say no: x402 calls past the budget are refused before signing, a card purchase over the card budget is refused, and a scam deal the fraud screen scores 0.85 is refused with no approval and no charge. It prints a PASS table on every PR and makes no paid calls.
- Your scanned room rebuilt as a voxel dollhouse, with each piece at its real spot and in its real color.
- A layout solver that puts your actual furniture against the walls of the new home without overlaps or blocked doors, in three styles, with a fly-in animation.

## What we learned
- Agents are much easier to trust when every action is a row you can see, rather than a log line on a server.
- The useful agent-payment primitive is a per-agent budget with reserve and release, plus a fetch that returns the receipt. Signing a payment is the easy part.
- Contract-first planning (one data model doc, one schema file) let four lanes and their agents work in parallel against fixtures before the real pieces existed.
- Label simulated actions from the start. Retrofitting honesty into a demo is much harder.

### Spacetime
SpacetimeDB isn't an add-on in Moove, it's the only backend. The iOS app and the agent worker never talk to each other. Both subscribe to the same tables and write only through reducers. Agents coordinate through rows: vision fills in an item's details, then pricing sees a priced-pending item, then listing sees a priced sell item, then approval sees a pending approval, and each step writes an `agent_event` the app can show. Payments and refusals are rows too, which is what makes the live spending log possible. Shared rooms (`join_room` with a 6-character code) are designed for two phones reviewing the same inventory live. Authorization uses `ctx.sender`: owners, room members and a registered worker identity. Code: `spacetime/src/index.ts`, `docs/data-model.md`. **Honest status:** the app's table mapping is on `main` (#28) and live updates were checked against a local server, not yet on a device. Share / join a room for the two-phone demo is on `main` (#78), not yet tested on two phones.

### Apify
The pricing agent's comps come from Apify actors: `caffein.dev~ebay-sold-listings` for what things actually sold for, and `apify~facebook-marketplace-scraper` for local asking prices (`ivanvs~craigslist-scraper` optional). Runs are cost-capped (`maxItems`, `maxTotalChargeUsd`), outliers are dropped, and the estimate is the condition-adjusted median with a quartile range. Every query is cached, and replays are labeled `[REPLAYED]` so a flaky scrape never breaks the demo. For a new home from a Zillow link, an opt-in switch runs `maxcopell~zillow-detail-scraper` (one result, cost-capped, cached per listing) to get the facts and floor-plan photos; it's off by default because scraping Zillow is against its terms. The buy agent's live deal search adds `ivanvs~craigslist-scraper-pay-per-result` and `automation-lab~ebay-scraper` (#40), with the same caps and a labeled replay of the last live search. Code: `agents/src/integrations/apify.ts`, `agents/src/integrations/deals-apify.ts`, `agents/src/agents/pricing.ts`. We didn't publish our own Actor, so we're not entering Best Actor.

### Visa: Reimagining Shopping
Moving is a shopping problem: the new place needs a bed frame, a desk and a shower curtain, and you don't want to spend a weekend comparing listings. Moove's buy agent works from the new home's floor plan, minus what you're keeping, to draft what's still missing. It ranks deals by price, condition and distance, and screens each one for fraud risk: price outliers, off-platform payment asks, new sellers and duplicate listings, each with a reason the app shows. It won't buy a deal scoring 0.7 or higher, and a deal that hasn't been screened yet waits for its score. In the demo, a planted and labeled scam deal on the Desk chair item ("Herman Miller Aeron, brand new, $95, Zelle only, ship only") scores 0.85, and choosing it is refused with no approval request and no payment. Then it checks out through a Visa Acceptance (CyberSource) sandbox flow: an authorization hold, a re-check of your approval and the current threshold, then capture, or a void if anything changed. **Trust & responsibility:** anything above your threshold needs your approval, the amount you approved must match what's charged, one purchase per item, marketplace sellers get a drafted message instead of a payment, a card budget and per-purchase max are enforced in code, a hold left open by a worker restart is reversed, and every step is visible. CI's end-to-end test proves the card budget refusal and the fraud block on every PR. What you buy shows up in the new home's 3D layout as voxel models. **Honest status:** real sandbox calls are signed and accepted, but the sandbox merchant has no card processor enabled yet (HTTP 502 SYSTEM_ERROR, #15), so the demo checkout is labeled SIMULATED. The VIC (Visa Intelligent Commerce) path is a documented seam only. The iOS commerce screens are not yet verified on a device.

### Auth0 (MLH)
Auth0 for AI Agents is the human-in-the-loop for spending. When the listing agent wants to post something above your threshold, the worker sends an Auth0 async authorization (CIBA) request, which is a Guardian push to your phone with a short binding message like `Moove: sell 420.00 USD: Herman Miller chair` (the amount comes first, so a scraped title can't read as the price). It polls for your answer, checks the token's subject matches you, and only then proceeds. A worker restart doesn't ask you twice, and a tap just before the request expires still counts. If push isn't set up, the request waits in the app's Approvals inbox. The app signs in with Auth0 Universal Login (#25), and the ID token becomes your SpacetimeDB identity. Code: `agents/src/integrations/auth0.ts`, `agents/src/agents/approval.ts`. **Honest status:** CIBA is tested only against stubbed HTTP. A real tenant run is pending. Login and `register_user` are wired to the live backend (#28), but not yet demoed on a phone.
