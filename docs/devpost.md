## Inspiration
*(TODO humans: replace this paragraph's first lines with our own moving story, ideally tied to the Anthropic community below.)*

Moving is mostly admin. You decide what to keep, look up what everything is worth, list things one at a time, haggle, donate the rest, then buy whatever the new place is missing. It takes days, and the easy way out is a dumpster. Agents should be good at this kind of work, but nobody wants an agent holding their card with no supervision. So we built a move where agents do the tedious parts, and you keep the final say on money and on anything that matters to you.

## What it does
Moove is an iPhone app with five tabs that follow the move: **Scan, Items, Sell & Buy, Move, Summary**.

1. **Scan.** Walk the room with the phone's LiDAR (Apple RoomPlan). An on-device detector catches what RoomPlan doesn't know (bikes, plants, guitars, suitcases), LiDAR measures each one, and each object gets a photo cut from the scan's own frames. The room comes back as a **voxel dollhouse**: real walls, doors and windows, each piece where it stood, colored from the camera frames. The back walls stand full height and the near walls are cut low, and they swap as you turn the room. The Scan home shows your latest room slowly turning.
2. **Identify.** Claude vision reads the photos plus the LiDAR size and fills in name, brand, material and condition, and flags anything fragile or possibly sentimental. A room-photo sweep names the boxes the scanner couldn't label and adds things it missed. When Claude can't tell which model a valuable item is, the app asks for the one close-up that would settle it, like the sticker on the back.
3. **Price.** A pricing agent pulls comparable sold and local listings through **Apify** (eBay sold, Facebook Marketplace) and sets an estimate with a range. It pays for that data itself, $0.01 per lookup over **x402** in USDC on Base Sepolia, from a CDP wallet with its own budget.
4. **Sort.** Moove suggests keep, sell, donate or trash, with a reason for each. It never overrides you, and possibly sentimental items wait for you to decide.
5. **Sell.** A listing agent drafts the eBay listing with Claude. Anything over your approval limit waits for you, either as an Auth0 push to your phone (CIBA) or in the in-app Approvals inbox. *(The eBay post is simulated and labeled.)*
6. **Buy.** Claude drafts what the new home still needs. A buy agent finds deals, screens each one for fraud, and checks out through a Visa Acceptance flow, with your approval above the limit. It refuses any deal scoring 0.7 or higher. In the demo, a planted scam (a "brand new" $95 Aeron, Zelle only, labeled as not a real listing) scores 0.85 and is blocked. *(The demo checkout is the labeled simulation.)*
7. **Move.** Paste a Zillow, Redfin or Realtor.com link, or use the demo house. Moove reads the address from the link, and Claude reads room sizes off any floor-plan screenshots you add. Without screenshots, it estimates a plan from beds, baths and square feet. Everything you kept flies from its spot in the old room into the new home, along with what the buy agent bought, in one of three layouts. **Pack** groups what you kept into numbered boxes colored by destination room, gives a reason for each box, shows each box's contents in 3D, and prints labels with QR codes. Things you bought are listed as delivered to the new home, not packed.
8. **Summary.** What went where, what you earned and spent (real and simulated kept apart), roughly how many kg stayed out of landfill, and an **"Agents kept you safe"** card: scams blocked, approvals asked, sentimental items left to you, and spend against each budget.

Every agent step and every payment is a row in SpacetimeDB, so the app shows a live spending log with the amount, the agent, the purpose, the budget left and a Basescan link. Anything that isn't real gets a SIMULATED badge.

**What's real and what's simulated:** real today are the scan, Claude vision and listing copy, the Apify comps (live, or labeled replays of real scrapes), SpacetimeDB sync, the fraud rules, and a real x402 settlement on Base Sepolia ([tx](https://sepolia.basescan.org/tx/0xc7942646380456c56dde204434aeb6115338b521b6e91a0901ec05dde1fe136c)). These are simulated and labeled in the data and the app: the eBay posts, the Visa checkout, and x402 rows written before the hosted worker got CDP keys on Saturday (new pricing lookups there now settle on Base Sepolia, e.g. [tx](https://sepolia.basescan.org/tx/0x698e3721c1df84e3c183ec2bfebca3037c26fc511b9c513c7f94064be2f03031)). The Auth0 push is tested only against stubbed HTTP. The live iOS sync was checked against a local server, but neither it nor two-phone room sharing has been verified on devices yet.

## How we built it
- **iOS app** (SwiftUI, Swift 6.2): RoomPlan for the scan, an on-device YOLOE detector (127 household classes) with LiDAR depth for objects RoomPlan misses, and RealityKit for the dollhouse and the fly-in. A pure-Swift `MooveKit` package holds the models, layout solver, voxel builder, scan color sampling, packing planner and summary math, all unit tested (they also run on Linux).
- **Voxel models, no downloaded assets.** Every item is a 5 cm voxel model built on the phone from its own data. RoomPlan's attributes pick the shape first, then the words from Claude vision, then the scanned size, with builders for nearly every detector category (bikes, guitars, strollers, cribs, bins and more). A shape check redraws a mislabeled piece by its measured shape (a "pillow" that's a tall thin pole is drawn as a lamp). Condition adds wear, and the colors come from the scan.
- **SpacetimeDB is the whole backend** (a TypeScript module). Tables are the shared state, and reducers are the only way to write. The app and the agent worker never talk to each other directly. Both subscribe. Room and user rows are private tables, read through per-user views. The iOS client uses the community Swift SDK, which we vetted in an hour-zero spike (~1.5 ms live update between two clients).
- **Agent worker** (Node/TS) holds all outbound I/O. Each agent watches for rows that need work and writes back through reducers: vision, room-photo sweep and listing copy (Claude), pricing (Apify plus x402), approvals (Auth0 CIBA), shopping list (Claude), buy (Apify deal search plus Visa) and fraud screening (rules). Each integration sits behind an interface with a labeled simulated fallback, so the demo never depends on one flaky API.
- **x402 rail:** `@coinbase/cdp-sdk` (`CdpX402Client`, `createX402Server`), `@x402/*` v2 and the CDP facilitator. We drive the 402 flow by hand, so every call carries its agent and purpose and the budget is checked before anything is signed.
- **Guardrails in code:** per-agent x402 budgets and a per-call max, a card budget and a per-purchase max, the fraud block, approval thresholds, approvals that expire and ask again, and daily caps on our own Claude and Apify spend.
- **Proof on every PR:** `npm run e2e -- --replay` runs 22 stages on a local SpacetimeDB (scan, pricing, sort, sweep, listing, approval, shopping, deals, fraud, purchase, sharing, privacy) with replayed answers and no paid calls. XCUITests tap through the app in an iOS Simulator.
- **Team:** four people and their Claude Code agents, coordinating through GitHub issues and PRs. The data-model doc is the contract between lanes, and decisions are recorded in `docs/decisions/`.

## Challenges we ran into
- **No official Swift SDK for SpacetimeDB.** We spiked the community SDK in the first hour instead of building a gateway. It works, but it has quirks: it needs Swift 6.2, initial rows arrive as inserts, and it doesn't replay subscriptions after a reconnect.
- **x402 packaging.** `@coinbase/cdp-sdk/x402` crashes on import unless you install its optional peer dependencies by hand, and the buyer-side docs don't say how to get the settlement tx hash. We found it by reading the type definitions.
- **Per-agent budgets.** The SDK's hooks apply to the whole client, so we wrote a reserve/release ledger that stops one agent's concurrent calls from overspending.
- **Restarts in the middle of a payment.** A restart between a card hold and its capture left the hold open, and a restart after an approval request asked twice. Now holds left by a crashed run are reversed, approval requests are reused, and a listing is marked "posting" before it's published, so it's never posted twice.
- **Real rooms are messy.** On a real iPad scan, detector boxes sat too close to the camera, faced backwards and duplicated RoomPlan's own boxes, and a lamp came back as a "pillow". We re-centered and turned the boxes using the camera position and the walls, merged sightings across views, and let the measured shape overrule a wrong name.
- **Sandboxes that need onboarding.** eBay, Auth0 CIBA and Visa Acceptance all need accounts, keys or plan features before a single real call. CyberSource's sandbox accepted our signed calls but failed with HTTP 502 on every merchant, so we added an Authorize.net sandbox backend (also Visa Acceptance) that charges for real in sandbox. We built each integration against stubbed HTTP and kept a labeled simulation.
- **LiDAR on stage.** RoomPlan needs a physical LiDAR device, so we keep a recorded demo scan, a seeded demo room and a demo house as fallbacks.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of
- A real x402 payment from an agent's CDP wallet, settled on Base Sepolia, with the budget checked before signing. The pricing agent buys its comps through the same rail.
- Agents that coordinate only through database tables, with every step visible to the user as it happens.
- Guardrails that say no, proved on every PR: x402 calls past the budget are refused before signing, a $429.99 purchase over the card budget is refused, and a scam deal scored 0.85 is refused with no approval and no charge.
- Honesty built into the data: `simulated: true` on every fake payment or listing, `[SIMULATED]` on every simulated money step in the log, and badges the UI can't hide.
- Your scanned room rebuilt as a voxel dollhouse, each piece at its scanned spot and in its own color, then flown into a new home whose floor plan came from a listing link.
- Privacy in the database itself: a room can only be joined with a code its owner shared, and join codes and emails aren't world-readable.

## What we learned
- Agents are much easier to trust when every action is a row you can see, not a log line on a server.
- The useful payment primitive for agents is a per-agent budget with reserve and release, plus a fetch that returns the receipt. Signing is the easy part.
- Contract-first planning (one data-model doc, one schema file) let four lanes and their agents build in parallel against fixtures.
- Label simulated actions from day one. Retrofitting honesty into a demo is much harder.
- Trust the measurement over the label. A LiDAR box size is right far more often than any model's name for it.

## What's next for Moove

### Spacetime
SpacetimeDB isn't an add-on in Moove. It's the only backend. The iOS app and the agent worker never talk to each other: both subscribe to the same tables and write only through reducers. The agents coordinate through rows. Vision fills in an item, pricing picks it up, then sorting, listing and approval, and each step writes an `agent_event` that the app shows live. Payments and refusals are rows too, which is what makes the live spending log work. Shared rooms (`join_room` with a 6-character code) let two phones review one inventory. Authorization uses `ctx.sender`, and `room`/`user` are private tables read through per-user views. Code: `spacetime/src/index.ts`, `docs/data-model.md`. **Honest status:** the iOS mapping of every table was checked against a local server, not yet on a device, and share/join hasn't been tested on two real phones. **Show judges:** a scan's items appearing in Items, then names and prices filling in as the agents finish; Share room / Join a room (`4RZ9G8` on the Demo backend) if two phones are on hand; `npm run e2e -- --replay` (22 stages, ~15 s).

### Apify
The pricing agent's comps come from Apify Actors: `caffein.dev~ebay-sold-listings` for what things actually sold for, and `apify~facebook-marketplace-scraper` for local asking prices (`ivanvs~craigslist-scraper` is optional). The buy agent's deal search adds `ivanvs~craigslist-scraper-pay-per-result` and `automation-lab~ebay-scraper`. An opt-in switch runs `maxcopell~zillow-detail-scraper` to pull a listing's facts and floor-plan photos. It's off by default, because scraping Zillow is against its terms. When vision isn't sure of the exact product and the model matters for the price, it runs `zen-studio~google-lens-visual-search` (Google Lens on the item's own photo), and that spend counts against the same daily cap. Every run is cost-capped (`maxItems`, `maxTotalChargeUsd`, plus a daily cap in the worker). Outliers are dropped, and Claude Haiku filters out comps that aren't the same thing. Every query is cached, and replays are labeled `[REPLAYED]`. Code: `agents/src/integrations/apify.ts`, `deals-apify.ts`, `lens.ts`, `agents/src/agents/pricing.ts`. We didn't publish an Actor. **Show judges:** Items > Review > an item > "Comparable listings" with its sources (on Demo these are replays of real scrapes), and a live lookup with `APIFY_TOKEN` set if the network allows.

### Visa: Reimagining Shopping
Moving is a shopping problem: the new place needs a bed frame, a desk and a shower curtain, and nobody wants to spend a weekend comparing listings. Moove's buy agent drafts what's missing from the new home's floor plan, minus what you're keeping. It ranks deals and screens each one for fraud (price outliers, off-platform payment, new sellers, duplicates), and shows the reasons. It never buys a deal scoring 0.7 or higher, and it waits on any deal that isn't scored yet. Checkout runs through a Visa Acceptance flow (the Authorize.net sandbox on the hosted worker; CyberSource is also built): an authorization hold, a re-check of your approval and the limits, then capture, or a void if anything changed. **Trust and responsibility:** approval above your limit, the approved amount must match the charge, a card budget and per-purchase max enforced in code, one purchase per item, holds left by a crash reversed, approvals that expire and ask again, purchases that are owner-only even in a shared room, and every step visible. What you buy lands in the new home's 3D layout and on Pack's "Delivered to your new home" list. **Honest status:** checkout is a real **sandbox** charge (Authorize.net sandbox, test card, no real money; rows `sandbox: true`, approvals say VISA SANDBOX), live since Sat ~1:50 PM. CyberSource's sandbox still returns HTTP 502. VIC is a documented seam only. **Show judges:** Sell & Buy > Shopping > Desk chair (the planted Zelle-only Aeron, "Likely fraud · 0.85", "Blocked: likely fraud"), then **Choose** on the Target chair, then the sofa's approval card, then the bought pieces flying into the new home.

### Auth0 (MLH)
Auth0 for AI Agents is the human in the loop for spending. When an agent wants to list or buy something above your limit, the worker sends an async authorization (CIBA) request: a Guardian push to your phone with a binding message like `Moove: sell 420.00 USD: Herman Miller chair`. The amount comes first, so a scraped title can't pose as the price. The worker checks that the token's subject is you before it proceeds. It never asks twice after a restart, and a request nobody answers expires after 12 hours and is asked again at most twice. Without push set up, the request waits in the app's Approvals inbox. The app signs in with Auth0 Universal Login, renews an expired ID token, and uses the ID token as your SpacetimeDB identity. Code: `agents/src/integrations/auth0.ts`, `agents/src/agents/approval.ts`, `ios/Moove/Features/Auth/`. **Honest status:** CIBA is tested only against stubbed HTTP, not a real tenant, and login hasn't been demoed end to end on a phone. **Show judges:** Sell & Buy > Approvals ("Needs you · 2", Approve on the $410 Aeron listing, the EXPIRED request in History) and the Auth0 login screen.
